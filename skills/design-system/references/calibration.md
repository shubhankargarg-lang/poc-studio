# Calibration · what the team judged strong and weak

The creative director judges against this file (references/creative-director.md, Part 2). It holds the team's taste,
so that a run without people in the loop still judges the way they would. Each entry describes **qualities** to judge
by. It is never a design to copy: the entries name past clients only so the team can find the files again.

**Add to it after every review round with the team.** One entry per design system they judge: verdict, the reasons
in their words, and what to look for next time. Keep entries short; the reasons matter more than the description.

---

## The team's standing feedback (applies to every run)

- Rich and polished, never plain or bland. When references are given, their ideas are visibly used.
- The light and dark switch re-themes every screen and every specimen; nothing stays stuck white or black.
- The client's real, official logo, never a redrawn or stand-in mark (the one exception is in SKILL.md stop 2).
- No "demo", "synthetic", "dummy" or placeholder labels anywhere. Real names, real numbers from the seed data.
- The brand is the client organisation that uses the app, never a sub-brand or a range it works on.
- Each new app must look like no app we made before.
- Show the thinking: variants to choose between, hover and selected states drawn, gradients and depth from the brand,
  more than one view of the main data, navigation explored. Short is good; plain is not.

## Generic tells (name each one you see)

- Components shown on their own on grey, never inside the client's real work.
- The hero object reduced to text when users judge it by eye (a product as a name in a row; a site as an address).
- Documentation outweighs design: long tables of tokens, parts and rules before anything worth looking at; the
  same row drawn again and again to show its states.
- A cover that is a table of where the file came from.
- The house look recoloured: the same shell, surfaces, type and spacing as earlier apps with a new accent.
- A default font chosen because it was safe (Inter, system UI) when the brand's own type has more character.
- The same four stat tiles across the top; a list with a right rail of summary cards on every page.
- AI sparkles or gradients on every AI element; a gradient banner with no reason from the brand.
- Empty states with a generic icon and "No data yet".
- Every card the same size in a uniform grid; icons decorating labels that need none.
- The idea named in the direction but visible nowhere on screen.
- Too quiet: black, white and grey with one thin accent; no gradient, depth or colour moment anywhere.
- One answer per component: a single variant, hover and selected states not drawn.
- No flows: single components and full screens, with nothing showing how the components do a job together, and no
  principle that says why the job is designed that way.
- One view per data set: the main list shown only one way; no board, table, timeline or gallery alternative.
- The same navigation as every app: a left rail or a top bar chosen without trying others.

## Entries

### Strong · Pentland Brands, Product Design System (human-iterated, v1.2)
File: `Pentland_Product_Design_System.html`. Judged by the team as clearly better than the first automated run.
- **It shows the work, not the parts.** Components appear inside real Pentland moments: a line plan board where
  each drop's columns hold the actual garments, an asset library with a detail pane, a creative viewer with safe
  areas and channel checks, fabric cards with composition, weight and price, a bill of materials with grouped
  rows. Each moment teaches several components at once.
- **The hero object is pictured everywhere.** Garments, boots, swatches and campaign images are drawn in the page,
  sitting on white wells in both themes so colour judgement stays honest.
- **A short, specific visual thesis.** The brand's black carries the navigation, one accent marks the single action
  that matters, AI content has three intensities (surface, content, action) with rules for each.
- **Compact and confident.** About 5,000 words and 13 tables across 28,000 px. Rules are one or two lines beside the
  thing they govern.
- **Flows at component level.** About fifteen flow panels grouped under five jobs ("Stage gates, samples and the
  critical path", "From tech pack to channel-ready product data", "Large creatives and every locale side by side",
  "Readiness, assets and variants at scale", "From fabric to passport"). Each panel is one job done by several
  components together (the line plan board, the tech pack viewer, the creative viewer, the product page preview with
  its retailer checks), live with real products, followed by three principle cards ("Preview the channel, not the
  record", "Every failure points at the page", "Fix where it fails"). This is where the reviewer saw the thinking.
- **Its own type.** A display face with character (Archivo) with a mono for codes, never a default sans.
- Look for next time: the first screen already shows the client's products and the one idea; any reader can scroll
  it like a product site.

### Weak · Pentland Brands, first automated run (design system v11.3)
File: `pentland-brands-design-system_new_version.html`. Judged by the team as "sloppy, not up to the mark".
- **A manual, not a design.** About 24,000 words, 91 tables and 108,000 px: every component got twelve parts at
  the same depth, so nothing stands out.
- **Text where the business judges by eye.** The signature was a timeline of style names; product pictures appeared
  almost nowhere, for a fashion and footwear company.
- **Components in isolation.** Grey specimens, state matrices that repeat one row six times, no composed moments.
- **The house look.** Its shell, surfaces and spacing matched earlier apps with a new accent; the font fell back to
  Inter.
- **A metadata cover.** The first view was a table of source files.
- Look for next time: if the first view could be swapped onto another client's file with only the logo changed,
  it fails.

### Mixed · Pentland Brands, Enrichment Workbench (design system v12)
File: `pentland-brands-design-system_new_new.html`. The team's verdict: a big step up, but the thinking is missing.
- **Good:** the listing proof drawn as the real Amazon UK page with numbered marks; a wall of real Berghaus products;
  short (6,100 words, 6 tables, 31,000 px); looks like Pentland and like no earlier app.
- **Missing, in the team's words:** "no variations"; the number and variety of components went down (13 variants
  where the run before had 23; 6 hover states drawn where it had 57); "use variations of gradients, hover states";
  "the in-depth exploration is missing"; the original had more view types and page types; the same navigation
  everywhere, "no innovation in the side bar or nav bar".
- **Data slips a reviewer will catch:** 38 active styles on one screen, 40 on another; on-model shots mixed into a
  wall of packshots; a peach taken from an unverified colour lead.
- Look for next time: count the variants and the drawn hover states; look for the views section and the navigation
  options; check every number that appears on two screens.
