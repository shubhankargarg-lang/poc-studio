# Where to look, by kind of question

Pick the row that matches the question. Start with the first source type in the row.

## Contents
- Company or client
- Industry process, roles and terms
- Regulations and standards
- Products and competitors
- Benchmarks and market figures
- Design and brand references
- Internal knowledge

## Company or client
**Look at:** the company's own site (about, products, newsroom, investor pages), annual reports and
filings, established news, and the company's public statements on the topic.
**Watch for:** old pages. Check the date, and check that the entity matches (similar names, parent
and subsidiary companies).

## Industry process, roles and terms
**Look at:** industry associations, standards organisations, practitioner guides, vendor
documentation that explains a process, and professional bodies for role descriptions.
**Watch for:** vendor guides that describe the process the way their product does. Compare two
sources before calling a step standard.

## Regulations and standards
**Look at:** the regulator's or government's own site, official registers and gazettes, standards
bodies.
**Watch for:** the country. Rules differ by market, so research the one where the pilot runs. Note
the date of the version you read, since rules change.

## Products and competitors
**Look at:** the vendor's product and documentation pages, release notes, pricing pages, analyst
summaries, and independent reviews.
**Watch for:** outcome claims on product pages and case studies. Report them as the vendor's claims.
Search each product separately.

## Benchmarks and market figures
**Look at:** industry bodies, analyst firms, government statistics, academic papers, and published
surveys with a stated method.
**Watch for:** the definition behind a figure (what is counted, which period, which region) and the
date. Use the same definition when comparing numbers. Confirm a key figure with a second source.

## Design and brand references
**Look at:** the client's public brand or press pages, design galleries (Dribbble, Behance, Mobbin,
Awwwards), public product sites of comparable tools, and published guidelines (Material, Apple Human
Interface, WCAG for accessibility).
**How to use them:** collect specific references with a link each. Describe in your own words what is
worth borrowing, such as the layout idea, the density, the chart style or the colour balance. Prefer
rich, polished references with real data and clear hierarchy over plain wireframes. Do not copy or
recreate a specific shot, a logo or a brand's artwork. A reference shows an approach that the design
reinterprets.
**Watch for:** gallery sites that need a real browser session. Use a connected browser for them.

## Internal knowledge
**Look at:** the team's stored knowledge from earlier work (a knowledge repository, shared drive, or
connected workspace) when one is connected.
**Watch for:** confidentiality. Internal findings can inform the answer. They stay out of search
queries and are labelled as internal in the sources.
